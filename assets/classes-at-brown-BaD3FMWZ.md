Taking inspiration from some other people around Brown that have independently summarized their classes @ Brown, I thought it would be helpful to contribute my own thoughts! For context, I'm concentrating in comptuational biology on the computer science track focusing generally on genetics/molecular biology. 

For each class I am going to give a rating out of 5 primarily informed by the following criteria:
1. Personal enjoyment of the class
2. Utility of the content in the class
3. How much I believe a student mildly interested in content/professor should take the course

---
## Fall 2023

<details>
<summary>BIOL0190R: Phage Hunters I (4/5)</summary>
Intensive wet-lab freshman seminar that spanned entire freshman year. Fall semester focused on learning and applying techniques for isolating bacteriophages from soil samples. Generally, I spent 4-6 hours in lab each week voluntarily. Evidently, I am not as involved with wet lab work these days but I am very glad to have experienced the small group settings of a freshman seminar. I am even more grateful to have been in close proximity with Sarah Taylor who was our course professor. Incredibly knowledgeable about all things remotely related to the biology concentration and so helpful early on.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI0150: Introduction to Object-Oriented Programming (5/5)</summary>
First CS course ever! The actual stack of the class is admittedly outdated ([JavaFx stopped being supported in 2025](https://www.azul.com/blog/oracle-support-for-java-8-with-javafx-is-ending/)), but that's not the point. JavaFX is simple enough to give students experience with library programming, combing through documentation, and practice with fundamentals of program design that I've found universally applicable in all of the projects I undertake. More importantly, the course environment is the greatest I've been a part of at Brown equal parts fun, inviting, and rigorous in a way that convinces many people to want to return and keep building the culture! 

</details>

<details>
<summary>EDUC0300: Introduction to Education (3/5)</summary>
I was really interested in education systems coming out of high school since our local school district had many conversations about tracking and resource allocation during my senior year. By nature of being an introductory class, the class covers a lot of material in broad strokes. Lectures were generally engaging, but I found myself much more willing to do readings and be active when the topics aligned with my interests. I realized very quickly that I was truly interested in pedagogy and learning theory rather than the systemic considerations of education systems.
</details>

<details>
<summary>MATH0180: Multivariable Calculus (3.5/5)</summary>
Course was very straightforward and painless. I found that the textbook was the generally best way for me to learn content relative to lectures or engaging with recitation. I wish I took MATH0350 Multi with theory but was afraid to take on an excessively heavy courseload freshman fall. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>MUSC0630: Jazz Band (4/5)</summary>
Very fun once a week combo performing twice a semester. I found this to be a great way for me to get involved/stay in the jazz scene at Brown! Depending on my semester load, I sometimes find myself wanting our group to be more or less serious.
</details>

## Spring 2024
<details>
<summary>APMA1650: Statistical Inference I (4/5)</summary>
My iteration of the course moved slow relative to other years - only began hypothesis testing by the end of the year. Topics are all digestible and the class itself was fine. Prof. Culic was engaging and genuinely wanted students to succeed. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>BIOL1950S: Phage Hunters, Part II (4/5)</summary>
Second semester focused on genome annotation and developing a review paper of a topic of our choosing. Much more relaxed semester compared to the fall meeting once or twice a week for annotation + paper development. I personally enjoyed learning about some of the fundamental computational biology techniques such as sequence alignment. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>BIOL0500: Cell and Molecular Biology (3.5/5)</summary>
Class introduces fundamental topics in cell biology that has been helpful for understanding essentially all related work that I've engaged with since. Structure of the course itseful is essentially 3 exams where any content from the slides is fair game. I found that going to lecture was not helpful and reviewing the slides, making flashcards, and drawing out diagrams was more effective. Content is generally necessary knowledge in other bio classes but there's overlap with a few other intro level courses.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI0200: Program Design with Data Structures and Algorithms (4.5/5)</summary>
Straightforward course that was interesting and helpful, not very time intensive. I thought it was a good progression from cs15 from me since it didn't hide many details of the concepts that were learned. I strongly believe that for most SWE internships/jobs the only prequisite knowledge is covered in cs15 and cs200 (plus the programming/debugging skills to apply the knowledge).
</details>

<details>
<summary>MATH0520: Linear Algebra (3/5)</summary>
Content is very foundational for many upper level courses. However, I find that the team based learning decreased some of my mastery of the content which I find myself relearning in other courses when required to use the fundamental concepts that were covered in thsi class.
</details>

## Fall 2024
<details>
<summary>BIOL0470: Genetics (4.5/5)</summary>
I loved the topics taught in genetics and the structure of the course/evaluation of knwoledge. Many concepts are heavily applied such that comprehensive understanding is required to problem solve but the knowledge base is not as broad as other biology/chemistry classes. Many people find that this style of course is difficult as evaluation presents itself in scenarios different than those on homeworks. Grading structure of course is very forgiving such that I did not need to take the final. I found that homeworks were the most useful for my learning compared to lectures/TA sections.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CHEM0330: Equilibirum, Rate, Structure(2.5/5)</summary>
Necessary evil to get to more interesting classes. Exams in the course were very rigorous and sometimes zeroed in on topics that didn't seem to be a focus in lecture. Content itself is survey style where it felt like a lot of content was being abstracted for the sake of moving fast. Made me want to come back to look into physical chemistry at some point. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI0330: Intro to Computer Systems (4/5)</summary>
Low level programming was very interesting and getting into the weeds is very thought provoking. Projects in the class were all relatively interesting and useful for understanding systems programming and design considerations involved. Course vibe was heavily carried by the fact that many sophomores in cs take this course in the fall. TWD was also funny!
</details>

<details>
<summary>ENGL0500P: The Examined Self (4/5)</summary>
Content of the course was very eye opening in the sense that it exposed me to so many different perspectives than I usually consider in my daily life/work. I appreciated Prof. Gould who was always spirited and passionate during lectures and helpful in office hours. Didn't necessarily like all of the readings but was willing to do them all since our in-lecture discussions were always very nuanced and insightful.

</details>

## Spring 2025
<details>
<summary>CHEM0350: Organic Chemistry I (4.5/5)</summary>
People who enjoy puzzles, spatial visualization, and pattern recognition will surely enjoy organic chemistry. The course begins by giving students some basic building blocks and specific reaction examples that are then generalized into reaction types and synthesis patterns, especially in Orgo II. I found that I did not need to spend as much time as many people in the class if I focused on pattern matching since there are a lot fo different reactions that can be memorized but relatively few patterns. Zimmt is also a great professor.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI0220: Introduction to Discrete Structures and Probability (4/5)</summary>
Enjoyed seeing how the first order logic and set theory was introduced as I had learned them on my own previously. The probability, combinatorics, and other content is review mostly from linear and/or stats. Also liked that the content was in the context of cs applications. Generally, I think the course does a good job giving a rigorous introduction for those with no background. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI1470: Deep Learning (3.5/5)</summary>
The course that got me started on my AI journey! By nature of the field right now, the course feels very survey based where we sometimes covered topics like VAEs and GANs in a single day. This is one of the few survey courses I enjoyed since I appreciated getting to generally survey the field in such a way that I could at least recognize the ideas that groups are applying at the industrial edge. However, I think for most students the structure isn't very productive, and often the assignments did not line up with the content that was being covered in lecture. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI1550: Probabilistic Methods in Computer Science (4/5)</summary>

Rigorous introduction to a variety of probabilistic concepts in cs from sample complexity to packet routing that that is very proof heavy. Prof. Upfal is good at explaining the intuition behind many concepts/algorithms and building them up formally which I enjoyed. Entirety of undergraduate level class was 5 problem sets that I spent a significant amount of time working on. Often the workflow was similar to lecture where an intuitive answer would need to be formalized into a rigorous answer. I found the course very fulfilling and a great exercise in transitioning from ideas to solutions though it was quite dense. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>NEUR1970: Independent Study (-/5)</summary>

I am not going to rank this since every independent study will be different. However, I strongly recommend undergraduates to get involved in research in some capacity during their time in university. Got credit for working with Dr. Saud Alhusaini on a clinical project studying essential tremors that I have been a part of since freshman year. 
</details>

## Fall 2025

<details>
<summary>APMA1080: Inference in Genomics and Molecular Biology (4/5)</summary>
Course primarily covers probabilistic/statistical modeling techniques for biological problems. However, the introduction/understanding of concepts do not require biological understanding or background. I took the class with Prof. Mallory who was taking over for Prof. Harrison who was on leave. I enjoyed Mallory's teaching but cannot comment about Harrison who is the original developer/professor of the course. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>CHEM0360: Organic Chemistry II (4.5/5)</summary>
Orgo II is more general than orgo I teaching mechanism/synthesis patterns and asking students to apply them often in new/different permutations relative to those covered in class. I am giving this a high rating since I think the fundamental problem solving skills cultivated are widely useful and applicable unlike other courses which I personally enjoyed more but I think are less generally useful.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI2952G: Deep Learning in Genomics (4/5)</summary>
Course is set up as a journal club where 1 or 2 students give an hour long presentation on the day's selected paper and a semester long final project. Selection and organization of topics felt intentional and I was able to more deeply explore many of the concepts that I had learned in Deep Learning. I also really enjoyed the focus on inrepreting models for validation and explainability! Personally a course that has set me on the path I'm on now. Ritambhara pulls this class together and is genuinely invested in student learning and encourages students to explore their offshoot interests. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>LITR0110A: Fiction I (4/5)</summary>
I was on a panel with [Dr. Dave Chokshi](https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/profiles/dave-chokshi) during my summer at MSK where he was asked what the greatest advice he ever recieved from a mentor was. His response was that someone had told him to read more novels. In medicine and in life, one of the most important skills is to be able to understand and empathize with the person across from you and reading novels and seeing the world from the perspective of these fictional characters encourages this kind of flexible thinking. Therefore, I wanted to try it out for myself and I was also interested in learning to write fiction! You get what you put into this course but I thought it was a great experience for myself personally.

</details>

<details>
<summary>MUSC0630: Jazz Band (4/5)</summary>
Very chill once a week combo performing twice a semester. I found this to be a great way for me to get involved/stay in the jazz scene at Brown! Depending on my semester load, I sometimes find myself wanting our group to be more or less serious.
</details>

## Spring 2026

<details>
<summary>BIOL0280: Biochemistry (3/5)</summary>
Course content is very in depth but really only useful for a subset of students at Brown. Found lectures not super useful though Holmes puts in a lot of effort to make the course engaging. Exams were fairly straightforward with a little more applied thinking than BIOL0500. In some sense, this course helps students develop their ability to learn/memorize from a fire hose which is useful in some fields.
</details>

<details>
<summary>BIOL1595: Artificial Intelligence in Health Care (4/5)</summary>
Course focuses on logistical and medical implications of designing, testing, and implementing decision support systems into existing healthcare workflows. Final project was fairly open ended. Sarkar was a good professor who is passionate and very supporting/accomodating of integrating personal interests into course programming. I think people interested in healthcare or applied AI would generally be happy in this course, especially since I feel that the persepctives of med-tech are a lot more conservative than other computational fields.
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI1420: Machine Learning (3.5/5)</summary>
Course gives a formal introduction to learning theory, covering different representations and sample complexity. Re-contextualized a lot of the content covered in CS1550 from a slightly different approach that helped me understand it better. Enjoyed the written homework more than the implementation. Quizzes did not get very deep into the weeds and were very straightforward. 
</details>

<details>
<summary>CSCI1851: Machine Learning for Biology and Health (4/5)</summary>
First iteration of class that Ritambhara developed. Course content itself mirrored a lot of machine learning while touching on biological applications. Really enjoyed the more openended projects that felt like research assignments and coming together to develop a final project. 
</details>
